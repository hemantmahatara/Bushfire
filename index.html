Method

  1. Five criteria reclassified to a 1–5 scale on a common 90 m grid (GDA2020 / Vicgrid).
  2. Weights from an AHP pairwise comparison matrix.
  3. Weighted overlay, then natural-breaks classification into five classes.
  4. Validated against DEECA fire history 1990–2025 and compared with 2009 and 2019–20 burn severity.

Reading the results

Burnt area rises from 17% of very-low cells to 68% of very-high cells, so the model ranks where fire has occurred. Fuel and slope carry most of the signal; the climate criteria point the other way because the wettest, coolest mountains are also the most forested and most often burnt. Severity is only weakly linked to susceptibility, which suggests fire weather on the day drives how hot a fire burns.
